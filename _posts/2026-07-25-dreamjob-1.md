## Scenario

Acting as a junior threat intelligence analyst, the task was to research a real, publicly documented cyber-espionage campaign — **Operation Dream Job** — using open-source threat intelligence (MITRE ATT&CK, VirusTotal) rather than a private evidence dump. Unlike a DFIR investigation, this Sherlock tests research methodology: knowing where to look, and how to correctly interpret what's published.

## Challenge overview

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Platform | HackTheBox Sherlock |
| Evidence | `IOCs.txt` — three SHA256 hashes |
| Research sources | MITRE ATT&CK, VirusTotal |
| Objective | Attribute the campaign and extract its documented TTPs |

## Phase 1: Attribution

Researching the campaign name directly against MITRE ATT&CK's group and campaign pages confirms **Lazarus Group** — a North Korea-aligned threat actor also tracked as APT38, Diamond Sleet, and Hidden Cobra — as the actor behind Operation Dream Job. The operation was first publicly reported by security researchers (ClearSky, McAfee) in 2020.

## Phase 2: Related campaigns

MITRE documents Operation Dream Job as an umbrella term covering two related campaigns:

- **Operation North Star**
- **Operation Interception**

## Phase 3: Technique research via MITRE ATT&CK

Working through the group/software pages on MITRE ATT&CK surfaces the specific techniques used:

**System Binary Proxy Execution** — the campaign abused two legitimate, signed Windows binaries to proxy execution of malicious code (a classic "living off the land" approach that blends in with normal system activity):
- `Regsvr32`
- `Rundll32`

**Lateral movement** — **Internal Spearphishing** (MITRE technique **T1534**) — rather than exploiting a technical vulnerability to move laterally, the adversary sent phishing content from within an already-compromised account to other internal targets, abusing internal trust.

**Remote Access Trojan** — **DRATzarus**, a RAT attributed to Lazarus Group and used specifically in this campaign.

**Sandbox evasion** — DRATzarus uses **Native API** calls (MITRE technique **T1106**) to query the host environment and determine whether it's executing inside a sandbox before proceeding — a standard anti-analysis check.

> The malware's specific *execution* technique (as distinct from its sandbox-check behavior) needs a direct check against DRATzarus's MITRE software page to confirm precisely — Native API is a strong candidate since MITRE technique entries frequently span multiple tactic categories, but this is flagged here rather than stated as fully confirmed.

## Phase 4: IOC analysis via VirusTotal

The provided `IOCs.txt` contained three SHA256 hashes. Submitting each to VirusTotal and cross-referencing the campaign's known tactics (job-themed lures targeting job seekers) ties directly back to the attribution above:

- **Third hash** — file name aligning with the campaign's known "fake job lure" tactic: `Salary_Lockheed_Martin_job_opportunities_confidential.doc`
- **Malicious URL** contacted by that file, used to fetch a secondary `.docx` payload: `https://markettrendingcenter[.]com/lk_job_oppor.docx` (defanged — remove brackets before use)

> The first and second hashes' specific VirusTotal details (malware family name, exact file creation timestamp, parent execution file) require direct lookup against the live VirusTotal report for full confirmation — this write-up notes the confirmed campaign-level findings above rather than asserting unverified specifics.
{: .prompt-warning }

## Key takeaway

This Sherlock is less about tooling and more about research discipline: correctly attributing a campaign, distinguishing between a technique's general MITRE description and its specific application by a named piece of malware, and being willing to say "this needs direct verification" rather than presenting an educated guess as a confirmed fact — the same standard that applies to real threat intelligence reporting.
